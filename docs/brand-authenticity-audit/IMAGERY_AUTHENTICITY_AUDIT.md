## 1. Verdict

**Yes — the imagery is an active contributor to the "feels like Kora" perception, and it fails in two different registers, not one.**

**Register A — the still-literal Kora stock (already flagged, confirmed here as maximally damaging).** The homepage hero is pixel-identical to `kora.framer.media`'s own hero: the same motion-blurred orange tulip against a cool blue-cyan sky, the same near-full-bleed crop, the same bottom-right teaser-card pattern (`home-fold0.png`, both sites). This is the single most visible pixel on the site and it is not "Kora-influenced" — it is *literally Kora's own unedited marketing photo*, sitting under headline copy that (per `EVIDENCE.md` item 1) follows Kora's exact sentence template word-for-word. A visitor doing a side-by-side comparison, which is exactly what triggered this audit, sees the identical photo before they've read a single word of copy. Six more Kora-stock service images and two Kora-stock article covers compound this (full list: `docs/agency-audit/MEDIA_AUDIT.md`'s Audit Table) — several are actively wrong-subject (a POS card-tap for *retirement planning*, a European-city group selfie for *child education*, a warehouse safety-vest headshot for *general insurance*), which reads less like "wrong brand" and more like "this template was never actually configured for this business."

**Register B — the "correct" AI imagery is still a different flavor of generic, and it introduces a problem the Kora stock never had: it invents a cast of people who compete with, and don't match, Ghanchi's one real, specific, named, photographed founder.** This is the sharper and more important finding of this audit, because Register A is already scheduled for replacement while Register B is currently marked "KEEP — already correct" in the prior audit, and on the narrow criterion that audit checked (ethnicity, palette, mood, aspect ratio) that verdict is accurate. On the criterion *this* audit was asked to check — does it read as *specifically Ghanchi*, not just *generically Indian-financial-warm* — it does not fully hold up:

- Every one of the 9 service cards renders a real, tiny (30×30px), circular photo of the actual founder — Chandrakant B. Ghanchi — captioned with his real name and role, floating in a glass-panel quote box **on top of** a large background photo (`src/components/home-sections.tsx:25`: `<Link className="service-visual image-hover">{service.image}<div className="service-quote glass-panel">...<Person image={founder?.headshot}.../></div></Link>` — both elements are children of the same card). For the Financial Planning card, that background photo depicts a *different, fictional* grey-haired senior advisor in glasses — a stand-in character invented by the AI generator, not Chandrakant. The real Chandrakant (dark hair, no glasses, a distinctive red tilak, captured in a phone-selfie-style headshot — see §4) is pinned in the corner of a card whose main photograph shows someone else standing in the advisor role his own quote and name are attached to. A visitor who has already met the real founder's face elsewhere on the same page (the 60px avatar permanently pinned in the header, `globals.css:101-104`) will register the mismatch, even subliminally: *that's not the same person.*
- Kora's imagery model — anonymous illustrative archetypes standing in for an abstract service category — makes sense for Kora because Kora *is* an abstract, multi-person system (6 named team members with distinct titles, a different named client quoted per service card, per `EVIDENCE.md`). Ghanchi is the opposite kind of business: every real testimonial captured in `evidence-data.json` names Chandrakant personally as the reason for trust ("Chandrakant is not an agent for me... but as a FAMILY member"; "Chandrakant has always taken the time to educate me"). Borrowing Kora's "invent an anonymous character for every service" imagery pattern — even after fixing the character's ethnicity and lighting — is applying the wrong pattern to a one-founder trust business. It's a warmer, more culturally appropriate version of the same category error, not a fix to the category error itself.

**Net effect**: the site currently spends its largest, most visible imagery real estate — the hero, and the big photo in every service card — on scenes with either zero connection to Ghanchi (Register A) or a fictional cast of stand-in people who visually compete with the one real person the business is actually built on (Register B). Meanwhile the one asset that could do the "this is genuinely Ghanchi" job — the real founder headshot — is confined to circular avatars no larger than 60px anywhere on the homepage (detailed in §4). The imagery hierarchy has it backwards: maximum pixel weight on invented/borrowed scenes, minimum pixel weight on the one authentic asset.

## 3. Per-slot findings and sharpened direction

Each entry gives the delta from `MEDIA_AUDIT.md`'s existing brief where one exists — not a restatement of it — plus explicit Subject / Environment / Lighting / Composition / Aspect-ratio direction where the sharpening changes the brief materially. Aspect ratios and CSS mechanics are as established in `MEDIA_AUDIT.md` (not re-verified here except where noted) — this section does not repeat that plumbing, only the *content* direction.

### I. Homepage hero (`site-setting.heroImage`) — highest-leverage slot on the site

`MEDIA_AUDIT.md`'s brief (a wide establishing/environmental scene, warm ambers, generous safe margins for the scroll-zoom and dark shade) is structurally correct and should stand as the technical envelope. What it's missing is the thing this audit was asked to check for: **nothing in that brief, as written, would produce an image a viewer could point to and say "that's Ghanchi" rather than "that's a warm financial-advisory stock photo."** A wide establishing shot of an anonymous sunlit office is exactly the same category of non-answer as the tulip — just recolored into the brand palette.

**Sharpened direction, in priority order:**

1. **OWNER / BUSINESS VERIFICATION REQUIRED — check this first, before commissioning any AI generation.** Does the business have *any* existing photo — even a phone photo, even low-resolution — of the actual office (Shop no. 27, Sector 11, Balaji Bhavan, CBD Belapur), or of Chandrakant at his desk with clients, taken wide enough to show environment as well as people? A real photo, even one needing crop/retouch/AI-assisted upscaling or outpainting to fit the hero's wide-canvas requirement, would out-perform any fully synthetic generation on the one criterion that matters most for this slot: it would be *true*, not merely *plausible*. This is the single highest-leverage place on the entire site to prefer a real photo over AI generation, precisely because it's the highest-visibility slot. Ask before generating.
2. **If no usable real photo exists**, the fallback AI brief should deliberately avoid putting an invented "advisor" face front-and-center in the frame — for the same reason given in §1's Register B finding: a synthetic close-up "advisor" face in the hero would be the most prominent instance of the fictional-person problem on the whole site, seen before anything else. Compose instead around **environment and hands/documents**, not a facial close-up: e.g., a wide shot of a consultation in progress where the advisor is partially turned away, seated at a desk mid-conversation, or cropped at the shoulders — present as a *role* in the scene, not a *face* competing with the real founder photo the header shows two inches away. Family/client figures can be more visible and expressive, per the existing content-truth policy allowance for anonymous illustrative scenes.
3. **Locality signals, specifically** (beyond "warm Indian interior," which any city's stock photography could produce): a CBD Belapur/Navi Mumbai commercial-district window view if outdoor-adjacent; small business-office details true to a small Indian financial-advisory practice — a visible LIC/insurer-branded document folder (generic branding only, never a fabricated specific insurer claim), a wall calendar, a Godrej-style steel almirah/filing cabinet rather than a Western open-shelf "startup office" look. These are the kind of concrete, checkable details that separate "a place" from "a mood."
4. **Technical envelope** (unchanged from `MEDIA_AUDIT.md`): minimum 3600×2000, ~16:9 or wider; generous safe margins upper-left (H1) and lower-right (newsletter teaser card); warm ambers/creams/browns, not the current cool teal; must read clearly under the `--ink #242424` dark shade with white text on top.

### II. The founder-mismatch problem — a cross-cutting flag, not a single slot

Documented in full in §1 (Register B) and §4. Applies to all 9 service-card slots identically, since the `service-quote` glass-panel + `Person` overlay pattern (`home-sections.tsx:25`) is shared code, not per-service content. **Recommendation for every service slot below**: do not generate a distinct, identifiable "advisor" character in the frame. Either compose the scene around the client/family with the advisor role implied but not facially prominent (partially turned, over-the-shoulder, cropped at frame edge), or drop the advisor figure from the scene entirely and let the small real-founder `Person` badge be the only "who's advising" signal in the card, as intended by the code's own structure — it's already designed to carry that job; the big photo doesn't need to compete with it.

### III. The 6 remaining Kora-stock service slots

`MEDIA_AUDIT.md` already gives correct subject/environment/lighting/aspect briefs for Mutual Funds, Retirement Planning, Employer Employee Insurance, Child Education Planning, Personal Accidental Policy, and General Insurance. The single sharpening that applies across all six, on top of that existing brief: **apply the §II flag** — none of these briefs should introduce a new distinct "advisor" character face. Two of the six existing briefs already lean this way naturally and should keep doing so:

- **Personal Accidental Policy** and **General Insurance** are the weakest candidates for any advisor-character scene (the existing brief for both already centers a client/document moment) — these are good candidates to go further toward the **prop/document-only register** validated in §2's table (an insurance policy folder, a house key or car key on a desk with a policy document, no faces at all) rather than a populated scene. This sidesteps the identity-mismatch problem entirely for two of the six slots and gives the newsletter cover's object-only approach (§V) a second application, which also reduces total AI-generation risk surface.
- **Mutual Funds, Retirement Planning, Child Education Planning, Employer Employee Insurance** keep their existing client/family-centered briefs from `MEDIA_AUDIT.md`, with the same instruction: if an advisor figure appears, keep them non-frontal/non-identifiable (back of frame, cropped, gesturing toward a document) rather than a posed, front-facing "advisor" portrait moment.

### IV. Article covers (2 slots)

`MEDIA_AUDIT.md`'s briefs (a composed Indian woman in quiet reflection for "Single Woman, Retiring Solo…"; an ordinary household/savings-passbook moment for "You Don't Have to Be Rich to Retire Rich!") don't introduce an advisor character at all — no §II flag applies here. The one addition: both briefs currently specify "warm home interior" with no locality anchor; apply the same light-touch Navi Mumbai/Maharashtra locality details suggested in §I.4 where they don't fight the subject (e.g., a wall calendar, a specific-but-generic Indian middle-class apartment window view) — optional polish, not a structural problem, since neither cover currently risks the identity-mismatch issue.

### V. Newsletter cover (1 slot, shared across all 19 archive entries)

`MEDIA_AUDIT.md`'s brief (an open notebook/ledger, a pen, a small growth-chart sketch — object-only, no person) is, on this audit's identity-continuity criterion, **the strongest brief in the entire prior audit**, precisely because it has no advisor character to mismatch against the real founder. No sharpening needed beyond what's already specified; flagged here as the model other slots (§III's Personal Accidental Policy / General Insurance) should follow.

### VI. Re-examination of the 3 already-"correct" AI images

This is the part of the brief the prior audit did not do — each image re-examined specifically for the "AI slop" and identity-mismatch questions, not just palette/mood/ethnicity.

**`ghanchi-service-financial-planning.png` — Financial Planning.** The strongest of the three on craft (real clutter — mismatched books, a coffee ring implied by the mug placement, uneven pendant-light falloff — genuinely reduces the "uncanny-smooth AI" feel). The identity-mismatch problem (§1, Register B) is most acute here: this is the one card where an invented senior advisor is doing the exact job — sitting at a desk, walking a client through a plan — that the real Chandrakant does, and the two men don't look alike (grey hair + glasses vs. the founder's dark hair, no glasses, distinctive forehead tilak). **Direction**: regenerate without a distinct advisor face in frame (compose from behind/beside the advisor, hands and the "Our Financial Goals 2024" mind-map document as the focal point, client reactions carrying the emotional beat) — the existing hand-lettered goals document is a genuinely good, specific prop; keep it. A minor secondary note: the blurred fourth person in the background is an unexplained, unnecessary element that adds visual noise without narrative purpose — drop it in any regeneration.

**`ghanchi-service-life-insurance.png` — Life Insurance.** No advisor figure at all — a father and daughter, candid, golden-hour, genuinely warm and specific in gesture (the piggyback, the laugh) rather than posed. This is the **best-aligned of the three** on the identity-continuity question precisely because it never tries to depict "the advisor" — it illustrates the *outcome* (family protected, joyful) rather than the service transaction. No sharpening needed on the mismatch axis. Only note: still lacks a locality anchor (§2 table) — the park could be anywhere; low priority given the image's other strengths.

**`ghanchi-service-health-insurance.png` — Health Insurance.** Two problems, one shared with the other cards and one unique to this image. Shared: the male figure is staged and dressed as **a physician** — white coat, stethoscope-adjacent framing, a framed medical "degree" certificate visible on the shelf behind him — reviewing what the scene presents as a policy document with a mother and daughter. This borrows a doctor's-office visual cliché wholesale, which is a second, independent source of "generic stock" feel beyond anything ethnicity or palette can fix: Ghanchi is an insurance and financial advisor, not a treating physician, and the scene as composed risks implying clinical authority the business doesn't have and doesn't claim (the actual service, per the site's own copy, is "Health insurance can help manage eligible medical expenses... coverage, exclusions, waiting periods and policy conditions" — an insurance-advisory conversation, not a medical consultation). Unique: this compounds the same identity-mismatch issue as Financial Planning — a third invented "professional" face (Financial Planning's grey-haired advisor and this doctor are not even styled as the same recurring stand-in character, let alone as Chandrakant), meaning the site's imagery currently has *three different fictional men* playing "the advisor" across three cards, none of whom is the one real person the whole trust proposition rests on. **Direction**: regenerate in an insurance-advisory register — business-casual or blazer, seated at a desk with an insurance policy booklet (mirroring the Financial Planning card's material language), no white coat, no medical-certificate prop — or, preferably per §II, drop the distinct advisor figure from the frame entirely and let the mother-daughter moment over the document carry the scene, consistent with the Life Insurance card's stronger approach.

## 4. The real founder headshot — how much the site actually uses it, and how much it should

`public/assets/ghanchi-founder-headshot.jpg` (Strapi media id 41) is a genuine, unposed, slightly imperfect photo: a front-camera-style close crop, a visible red tilak, a motion-blurred blazer edge, a ceiling light catching the frame off-angle — every quality that AI generation struggles to produce convincingly (§2's "photographic register" row) this photo already has for free, because it's real. It is, by a wide margin, the single most "genuinely Ghanchi" visual asset that exists in the project. The question this audit was asked is how much the *site* actually leans on that.

**Every place it currently renders, and at what size** (confirmed by reading the rendering code and its CSS directly, not estimated):

| Location | Component / CSS | Rendered size | Page |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header nav, "Get in touch" button | `site-shell.tsx:78`, `.header-call img` (`globals.css:103`) | **60×60px**, circular | Every page (persistent) |
| Every service card's pull-quote badge (×9) | `home-sections.tsx:25`, `.service-quote .person-image > img` (`globals.css:207`) | **30×30px**, circular | Homepage |
| Team section row (before click) | `home-sections.tsx:40`, `.team-row img` uses default `.person-image > img` (`globals.css:49`) | **48×48px**, circular | Homepage |
| Team dialog (after click) | `home-sections.tsx:40`, `.team-dialog-image > img` (`globals.css:234-235`) | Larger (~235px column, `min-height:380px`) but **hidden behind a click** — not visible on page load | Homepage (modal only) |
| Founder-callout badge | `home-sections.tsx:56`, default `.person-image > img` (`globals.css:49`) | **48×48px**, circular | Homepage |
| About-us founder feature | `about-us/page.tsx:16`, `.inner-founder-photo` (`inner.css:122`) | **Full-width column, 540px tall** (430px on mobile, `inner.css:268`) — the only genuinely large, primary-visual-weight rendering anywhere | `/about-us` only |

**Finding**: on the homepage — the page every "feels like Kora" comparison in this brief was made against — the real founder's face never appears larger than 60 pixels, and appears at that size only as a decorative circular avatar clipped into a "Get in touch" pill button, not as a deliberate "meet your advisor" moment. The only place the site gives the real photo room to actually function as a portrait — full column width, 540px tall, enough to read expression and setting — is on `/about-us`, a secondary page reached only by users who click through, and even there it's paired with copy (`page.founderIntroHeading`) rather than being the page's opening visual statement (the `/about-us` hero itself, `siteSettings.heroImage`, is the *same* Kora-stock/replacement hero image as the homepage, not the founder photo). Meanwhile the homepage's large-format visual real estate — the full hero and nine service-card backgrounds — goes to either Kora's own stock photo or invented AI stand-in characters who, per §3-VI, don't even look like the man whose 30–60px face is pinned in the corner of the same cards.

**Recommendation:**

1. **Promote the real photo to homepage-primary weight, not just secondary-page weight.** The "Meet your advisor" team section (`teamHeading: "Meet your advisor. A personal partnership."`) is structurally already the site's dedicated "who is Ghanchi" beat — it currently spends that beat on a 48px avatar behind a click-to-expand dialog. Consider giving this section (or the founder-callout section immediately after the testimonials) a large-format, on-page rendering of the real photo, matching or exceeding the treatment `/about-us` already gives it, so the homepage doesn't require a click to see the one authentic face on the site at meaningful size.
2. **The hero is the single biggest opportunity, and it's real-photo-first, not AI-first** (already flagged in §3-I.1): before commissioning any AI hero generation, check whether the business has — or can quickly get — a real wide photo of Chandrakant in his actual office. That photo, not a synthetic one, would be the most direct possible answer to "say this is a real founder, not warm mood" — the exact gap this whole audit was asked to identify.
3. **OWNER / BUSINESS VERIFICATION REQUESTED**: ask whether additional real photos of Chandrakant exist beyond this single headshot — at his desk, with a client (subject to client-privacy/consent consideration, since a real identifiable client photo would need explicit permission, unlike the anonymous-illustrative AI scenes elsewhere on the site, which the content-truth policy already covers), at an awards event (the site already has 8 real award photos in the `public/assets/ghanchi-dc1c1b_*` group and 19 real certificate scans — per `docs/MEDIA_INVENTORY.md` Group A — but none currently pair the founder's face with those achievements in a single frame). A small set of 2–4 real photos, used deliberately across the hero, the team section, and the founder-callout, would let the site stop depending on invented stand-in characters for every "who's the advisor" moment — replacing all three of §3-VI's fictional men with the one real one, everywhere that role appears.
4. **Production note, not a replacement question**: the existing headshot's selfie framing and modest resolution may need light professional retouching or upscaling for large-format hero-scale use (per the size table above, nothing on the homepage currently asks it to render larger than 60px, so this hasn't been tested) — this is a production step, not a reason to regenerate or replace the photo itself, which is already the authentic asset the rest of this audit argues for using more, not less.
